# Ruling 14 - LOADOUT.CFG MULTI-HUE PALETTE

*Added 2026-07-31. **USER DIRECTIVE**, recorded verbatim:*

> I'm wanting to see if we can break away from the single color design. I even said use Evangelion colors.

## What it decided

For the [[loadout-cfg]] instrument-panel study **specifically**, the KP/OS v2 single-ink-accent law is superseded by a five-channel state-coded palette:

| Channel | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| amber | `#ffab3d` | structural chrome, neutral readouts |
| green | `#7bff5a` | nominal: READY verdict, active mode |
| red | `#ff3b30` | risk: NEURAL STRAIN when low |
| cyan | `#35d6ff` | BENCH FEED camera tint only |
| ivory | `#fff2d9` | hot highlight, hero numerals |

## Scope: the narrow reading governs

This supersedes the single-ink law **for LOADOUT.CFG's own surface only**, the study and the shipped window it replaces.

- It does **not** reopen any other KP/OS window to multi-hue.
- It does **not** revive the earlier-rejected per-window channel livery (magenta darknet, phosphor ledger, indigo dadlog), killed by the v2 redesign round-2 ruling. That was flat single-hue-per-window, a different design from this window's internal state-coding by meaning.

## What it explicitly left OPEN

> [!question] Reserved for the user
> Whether colour-as-diagnostic-state should become the **general KP/OS law** in place of single-ink-accent everywhere, or stay a LOADOUT.CFG exception.
>
> **Until the user says otherwise, the narrow reading governs every other surface.**
