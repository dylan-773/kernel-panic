## What is being replaced

`kernel-panic-site/app/src/components/os/windows/night.tsx`, mounted inside
the flow window (`shop-os.tsx`, `run.screen === "upgrade"`, window title
`NIGHT.SYS`). There is NO existing `ui-demos/` study for this surface: it
was never rebuilt in the v2 cycle, so it is still wearing pre-v2 generic
chrome (`kp-upgrade`, `kp-screen-head`, `kp-patchrow`, `kp-regen` in
`styles.css`). Read the shipped component for STRUCTURE and state flow only.

Law 11 of `RULINGS.md` calls this panel out specifically: *"small surface; a
candidate for proving the system at low density."* **That is this study's
job.** LOADOUT.CFG proved the system can organise eleven blocks. NIGHT.SYS
has to prove it can carry six without the maximalism reading as padding.
Extreme contrast between what matters and what does not is the mechanism;
inventing filler to fill the panel is the failure mode. If the surface comes
out short, it comes out short: this is the one panel in the queue where
landing well under the height ceiling is a result, not a miss.

Current anatomy, a single stacked column, all of which must survive in some
form (or be cut on the record per law 8):

- **header**: the hero `DAY {n} CLOSED` numeral, plus a three-sentence
  paragraph ("One upgrade holds for the rest of the run. Pick it, spend your
  credits, then close the night. Nothing is locked in until you do.")
- **strain regen band**: a `STRAIN` label over a 30-segment meter that
  ANIMATES its fill from the pre-rest value 150ms after mount, with a
  `+{lastRegen} STRAIN` pop, silent when the meter was already full. This is
  the only animation the shipped surface has and it is its signature beat.
- **the upgrade grid**: four tiles, `ram` / `scan` / `attack` / `defend`,
  each a label plus a detail line plus a `SELECTED` stamp, disabled and
  relabelled `MAXED` at the cap. Reversible: picking does NOT end the night.
- **three shop rows**, each a ghost button plus a dim status line:
  - NIGHT PATCH: `+12 STRAIN` for `45 + 5*day` cr; dead at 100 strain or on
    short credits. Status: `STRAIN n/100 - n cr - rest restored +10`
  - BUY BLIND: opens DARKNET.LNK. Status: `POUCH n/5 - n cr - Pay first.
    Shape is the surprise.` plus the pouch glyph strip
  - INSTALL BOOST BAY: `150` then `300` cr, capped at 5. Status:
    `BAYS n/{boostSlots} - n cr`, a pip row to 5, and `A full bay drafts as
    a swap. More bays, more boosts held.`
- **footer**: the pointer line (either "Pick one upgrade above to close the
  night." or "Closing the night applies {X} and opens day {n+1}.") and the
  primary `CLOSE THE NIGHT` button, disabled until a pick exists.

Interaction that must stay live: the upgrade pick is a real, REVERSIBLE
choice (siblings demote, SELECTED stamps, the footer pointer line rewrites,
CLOSE THE NIGHT arms); the three purchases spend credits and move their own
readouts; and CLOSE THE NIGHT stays locked until a pick exists. Everything
else is readout.

## The v3 laws this surface has to answer

Every law in `RULINGS.md` applies. The six that bite hardest here:

1. **GLANCE ORDER (law 2), and this surface's is genuinely contested.**
   Three candidates. The spec must pick one and demote the other two
   explicitly rather than sizing them all at once:
   - the **DAY numeral**, which the shipped window makes the hero. It is a
     chapter marker: it says where the run is, and it is the one thing here
     that is pure state with no decision attached.
   - the **upgrade pick**, the only thing on the surface the player must ACT
     on, permanent for the rest of the run, and the thing blocking the exit.
   - **STRAIN**, the run's life, and the number the night's purchases exist
     to defend.
   A night screen whose focal element is the day counter is a defensible
   reading (it is the beat that says the day is over), but it has to be a
   DECISION, with the type scale built to enforce it: ONE focal element at
   extreme scale, 2x+ the next largest thing, annotation clusters at 9 to
   11px filling the space around it.
2. **HEIGHT CEILING (law 3), inverted.** ~700px target, 820px absolute,
   measured with the CDP harness in law 10. Unlike every other panel in the
   queue this one probably starts UNDER budget, so the risk is the opposite
   failure: a stacked column of six blocks that reads as a web form. Solve
   it with arrangement, not with additions. Law 8's closing lesson runs in
   reverse here and is the single most useful rule for this panel: **a row
   you do not share is a row you pay for in full**, so the three shop rows
   and the strain band are candidates to sit BESIDE something rather than
   under it.
3. **RISK (law 1): strain is the alarm, and note the polarity.** Strain
   counts DOWN as the run wears: 100 is fresh, 0 is out. Risk is LOW strain,
   the inverse of the usual meter read, so a nearly empty meter must not
   read as "nearly done". The reference build's bands are nominal above 70,
   neutral between, risk at or below 35; reuse them unless there is a reason
   not to, and state the reason. `--r-warn` appears on exactly one thing
   here: the strain readout in the risk band. It gets the second and third
   channels the law demands, inverse video AND motion, on the composited
   difference-blend pattern `loadout-eva` measured at frame parity (do not
   animate `background` or `box-shadow`). Ambient chrome stays dead still.
   The pip rows, the pouch glyph strip, the MAXED tiles and the "cannot
   afford" states must NOT reach for `--r-warn`; assign them `--r-struct` /
   `--r-note` / `--r-hazard` explicitly so nobody reaches for red twice.
4. **THE REGEN FILL IS THE SURFACE'S SIGNATURE MOMENT.** It is the one
   element here that no other window has: a meter that visibly fills as the
   window opens, because the shop closing IS the rest. Decide whether it
   earns focal scale, and specify the choreography exactly: what the meter
   shows on mount, when the fill starts, its duration and `steps()` count,
   what the `+10 STRAIN` pop does, and the branch where `lastRegen` is 0
   because strain was already full. Per law 7, reduced motion collapses to
   the final state in one frame with sound unaffected.
5. **EQUAL-FOOTPRINT EMPTY STATES (law 4).** This surface has more
   disabled-and-capped branches than live ones, and they are the normal
   late-run condition rather than an edge case: all four tiles can read
   MAXED, the pouch can be empty or full at 5, the bays can sit at the
   ceiling of 5 with no price to show, the patch button dies at 100 strain,
   and every purchase dies on credits. A late-run night where everything is
   capped must occupy the SAME footprint as a day 2 night where everything
   is live, or the window jumps every time it opens.
6. **THE PICK IS A STATE MACHINE, NOT A BUTTON ROW.** Whatever the glance
   order decides, the surface has to make three things unmissable: that
   exactly one of the four is chosen, that the choice is REVERSIBLE until
   CLOSE THE NIGHT, and that CLOSE THE NIGHT is locked until a pick exists.
   The shipped surface says the second only in a prose sentence and the
   third only by disabling a button. Corner-tick reticles are the house
   idiom for an active option (law 4) and `--r-ok` owns the active/selected
   option (law 1); use them rather than inventing a new selected treatment.

Role assignments worth stating in the spec: `--r-ok` owns the SELECTED
upgrade and the armed state of CLOSE THE NIGHT. `--r-data` owns the focal
numeral only. `--r-note` owns the `// LABEL` prefixes, unit captions, the
`cr` suffixes and the pouch shape nouns. Make every one of the eight
explicit, and if a role has no owner on this surface, SAY SO rather than
finding it a job: an unused `--r-warn` on a healthy run is correct, and so
is an unused `--r-aux` if the panel carries no imagery.

## Imagery: decide it, do not default it

This surface has no camera today, and `--r-aux` is the camera/imagery role.
Two defensible answers; the spec must pick one and say why:

- **No imagery.** The panel is a status-and-decision surface and holds
  without a picture. Then `--r-aux` goes unowned and the FEED variation does
  not exist. Law 8 step 1 backs this: imagery that cannot survive its size
  is cut outright rather than shrunk.
- **One diegetic cell**, if and only if there is an in-fiction reason for
  the OS to show it at day close (law 5). The bench camera at closing time
  is the obvious candidate and the raw exists at
  `_shared/art/raw/raw-solder-bench.png` (1024x1024). Any use is a CROP at
  1:1 of a re-dither of that raw, using the `pipeline/tools/dither.py` and
  colourisation generators recorded in `loadout-eva/NOTES.md`. Never
  downscale, never generate.

If imagery is in, the FEED variation (INK TINT / TRUE 1-BIT / FULL COLOUR)
comes with it per law 5. If it is out, say so in the spec's own words so the
reviewer sees a decision rather than an omission.

## Variations the study must offer (name them in the ui-spec)

- **SCHEME**: NERV (default) / TOKYO NIGHT. Copy the token block from
  `loadout-eva/index.html`; do not retype hexes.
- **CRT**: FLAT (default) / OFF. Six glass layers, copied verbatim. Do not
  rebuild CURVED.
- **VIEWPORT**: 16:9 1366x768 / 21:9 2560x1080 / laptop 1280x800, all three
  rendering the SAME arrangement.
- **RUN STATE**, covering every branch that changes the layout:
  - DAY 2 EARLY: strain at or near full so there is no regen pop, sparse
    pouch, 3 bays, every upgrade live, credits low enough that at least one
    purchase is out of reach
  - DAY 5 MID (default): the normal case, a visible regen fill, a mixed kit
    with one tier already maxed, a partly full pouch
  - DAY 8 STRAINED: strain in the risk band so the alarm's three channels
    are reviewable rather than described, and the night patch is the play
  - DAY 9 CAPPED: the late-run equal-footprint proof. All four tiles MAXED
    or nearly so, bays at the 5 ceiling with no price, pouch full at 5, so
    the fully disabled surface is checkable against the fully live one.
- **FEED** only if the imagery decision above says there is imagery.
- **REPLAY LOAD** for the load choreography, which must replay the regen
  fill as well as the panel stagger.
