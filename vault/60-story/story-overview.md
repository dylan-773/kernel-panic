# Story overview

Spoiler-free. For what is actually true, see [[ground-truth]].

## The premise

Your father is dead. He left you and your sister his computer repair shop, jointly: the counter to her, the bench to you. He also left a curtained, padlocked back room with a tower in it that he never let you touch.

[[rhea]] says it is a quarantined virus. She is certain. You are not.

## The shape of a run

Every run opens the same way. You dive the back-room machine, you are not ready, and it seals itself shut. Then you get to work: nine days, three tickets a day, diving customers' infected devices for pay. On day 10 the back room settles up.

## What the player is chasing

Two things at once, and they are the same thing:

- **Mechanically**, a kit good enough to beat the machine on day 10.
- **Narratively**, an answer to what the machine is and why he sealed it.

## The engine of the story

> [!info] Losing is the delivery mechanism
> Each failed run releases another fragment of the father, and advances [[rhea]]'s erosion of her own virus theory by exactly one step. `runCount` drives both. See [[reveal-schedule]].

This is the design's central move. The player is never punished with silence. A lost run costs credits and a day, and pays a chapter. See [[death-and-run-end]].

## The reveal, in outline

Runs 1 to 8 walk a fixed ladder: the debt, the illness, the nights in the back room, the recordings, the instruction, the naming. By run 8 the player can infer almost everything and [[rhea]] has dropped the virus theory. Knowledge then plateaus until a finale win, which delivers the last of it in the father's own voice.

## Tone

A shop, not a city. The cyberpunk is entirely peripheral: courier drones and clinic gateways arrive as tickets on a spike. The story is grief, debt, and a soldering lesson.
