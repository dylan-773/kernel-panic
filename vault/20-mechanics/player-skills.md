# Player skills

The skill system, such as it is. There are no skill trees, no XP and no levels.

Power has exactly three axes:

## 1. Programs

Three, always: [[scan]], [[attack]], [[defend]]. They never unlock and are never lost. See [[the-kit]].

## 2. Tiers

Each program independently 1 to 3, one bought per night. Tiers buy **width and range**: targets per cast, scan radius, ward radius, siphon steal. See [[program-tiers]].

## 3. Modes

Six, two per combat program. [[redirect]] and [[purge]] are free; the other four are unlocked by config [[augments]]. Modes buy **verbs**, not numbers.

| | default | unlocked |
|---|---|---|
| ATTACK | [[redirect]] | [[arm-halt]], [[arm-siphon]] |
| DEFEND | [[purge]] | [[lock]], [[ward]] |

## Why it is shaped this way

> [!danger] REVISED IN PROTOTYPE
> The draft had eight verbs and roughly twenty-four variants. It was unreadable at speed: players could not hold the catalog in their heads mid-duel, so they defaulted to one or two verbs and ignored the rest. Three programs with modes gives the same depth in something a player can actually consider each turn.

The result is a build space that is wide but never wide **in front of you**: at any moment you have three buttons, and the interesting question is what each is currently configured to do.

## Skill in the other sense

The player's real skill is routing: reading [[route-cost-and-par|route cost]], holding a chain for a [[cascades-and-surge|cascade]], and knowing which junction costs the machine most to repair. None of that is a stat. It is the same on day 1 and day 9.
