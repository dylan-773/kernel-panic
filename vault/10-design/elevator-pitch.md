# Elevator pitch

**Kernel Panic is a turn-based cyberpunk roguelike about inheriting your father's computer repair business and the machine in the back room he never let you touch.**

Your sister [[rhea]] runs the front counter and insists the locked machine holds nothing but a nasty virus. You are certain it is more.

Every run opens the same way: you dive it, you are not ready, it seals itself shut. Then you get to work, diving customers' infected devices to duel the intrusions inside. Each in-game day serves three tickets, and diagnostics on every job let you plan your order before committing resources.

Nine days of paying work. On day 10 the back room settles up.

## The hook

**Losing is how you learn who your father was.** Every failed run releases another fragment of him and moves your sister one step off her theory. The game has no punishing failure state because failure is the delivery mechanism.

## The combat

A duel on a grid of scrambled pipe junctions. You rotate junctions to route your signal to the goal; so does the intrusion, on its own board. You can reach across and cut theirs. Rotation only turns one way, so undoing an enemy twist costs three times what making it did, and that exchange rate is the whole economy.

## The frame

The entire game is an operating system. KP/OS boots, you log in, you drag windows around. There is no menu layer over the game because the desktop **is** the game. See [[kp-os]].

## Comparisons

The duel's read is closest to the Watch Dogs hacking minigame, turned into a two-sided race. The run structure is roguelike: seeded, ten days, nothing carries but knowledge.
