# LEDGER.LOG

`ledger` · `windows/ledger.tsx`

The accounting terminal. There is no score; there are books.

## What it shows

- **THIS RUN** against **LIFETIME**
- **CREDITS** as the hero number
- **MOST LETHAL**: a customer dossier
- Print furniture: the window is styled as a ledger printout

## MOST LETHAL

Whichever [[customers|customer]]'s device has ended the most runs.

> [!info] The only boss meter in the game, and nobody authored it
> It is emergent: the customer the player keeps failing becomes their nemesis without the game ever designating one. Two players will have different answers, and neither answer was written by anyone.

## Why books and not a score

A repair shop keeps books. It does not keep a high score.

Framing the record as accounting makes the number diegetic, which is the same move [[repair-log]] makes by reading a dive result as a transaction. Between them, the two windows mean the game never has to render an abstraction: everything the player is told about their performance is something the shop would actually have written down.

It is also the debt story. The ledger is where the money the shop does not have is visible. See [[the-shop]] and [[credits]].
