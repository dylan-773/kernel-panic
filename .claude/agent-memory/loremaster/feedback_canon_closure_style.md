When I proposed rulings for the 8 open canon questions (2026-07-25
session), on two of the highest-stakes items the user went further than my
recommendation rather than picking one of my listed alternatives:

- The mother: I recommended "deliberately left open" (never named, fate
  unknown, forbid future reveal). The user instead excised her from canon
  entirely: there is no "she", full stop, not even as an unresolved
  mystery.
- Timeline/dust: I recommended reconciling a "decade of dust" line by
  reading it as idiomatic. The user instead removed dust as a motif
  entirely and ruled it a rewrite of the shipped line, not a
  reinterpretation of it.

**Why this matters:** the user is willing to rewrite already-shipped copy
to serve canon, not just reinterpret it creatively within existing text.
When a tension exists between two shipped lines, "which reading avoids
contradicting shipped copy" is a good default heuristic for a *proposal*,
but the user may still choose the harder cut: delete/rewrite the
offending line rather than find a clever reading that preserves it.

**How to apply:** when drafting options for an open canon question,
include a plain "excise it / rewrite the shipped line" option explicitly,
even when a non-destructive reconciling reading exists, rather than
treating reconciliation as the obviously-safer default. Do not assume
shipped copy is sacrosanct when it conflicts with a cleaner canon.

This also generalizes to gates where no rewrite is on the table, only a
yes/no ruling on new fiction (e.g. `ui-integration-2026-07-29`'s INTRUSION
identity-tag and customer-appearance questions, see
[[project-canon-status]]): the user wants an actual decision recorded in
the lore files, not a hedge, a "flag for later," or an unresolved NOTE
when the gate has enough information to just rule.
