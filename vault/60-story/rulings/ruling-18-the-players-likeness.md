# Ruling 18 - THE PLAYER'S LIKENESS

*Decided 2026-08-19, gate ruling, closing the gap the walkable shop floor exposed. [[the-player]]'s old constraint, "never pictured, there is no player portrait asset," predates a room the player has to be seen walking.*

**What "never pictured" was always protecting: no portrait, no face, no close-up, no likeness a stranger could pick out of a lineup.** A small figure crossing a room at a fixed camera distance was never the thing that rule existed to stop. The rule is scoped, not repealed.

## Forbids

Any player portrait, dialogue bust, zoom in, loading-screen close-up, or promotional render. Any sprite state (idle, hurt, working the bench, walking) at a scale or angle where the face resolves. Any redraw of the canonical sprite below that lifts the hair, adds visible eyes, or otherwise lets the face read.

## Permits

A room-scale, full-body player sprite, seen only at [[the-shop-floor]]'s camera distance, whose face is never resolved: hair over the eyes, shadow, or too few pixels to carry a feature.

## The canonical sprite

Ruled acceptable under that test:

> Young man, worn olive work jacket over a dark hoodie, messy dark hair over his eyes, tired posture, tool pouch at the hip.

The hair is what makes this pass. At room scale it reads as "a guy at a bench," not as anyone in particular, and nothing in the description asks an artist to resolve a mouth, a nose or eyes. It stays legal at every distance the room's camera holds because the face was never drawn to hold up closer.

## Integration

[[the-player]] and [[player-character]] constraint lines are updated to cite this ruling in place of the old blanket "never pictured." Any future close-up treatment of the player (marketing, a cutscene, a still) needs a new ruling; this one covers room-scale only.
