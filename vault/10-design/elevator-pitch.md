# Elevator pitch

**Kernel Panic is a turn-based cyberpunk roguelite about inheriting your father's computer repair business and the machine in the back room he never let you touch.**

Your father passed away, and he left you his computer repair shop, the one you grew up in, watching him work and learning the ins and outs of battling viruses. He also left the machine he worked on in secrecy, day and night, but when you try to access it, you are locked out by what seems to be an extremely powerful virus. Was your father always in the backroom because he was trying to beat this thing, or something else?

Now left to bear the burden of one of the few honest businesses in this dystopian city, you greet customers, intake computers, and fix them to earn money. In this future, viruses are not how we think of them today. The boom in AI development from many years ago resulted in humanity creating something it can't quite control. Software became more like human brains, computers like sentient beings, instead of static rule-based systems. When the AI has our best interest in mind, it's wonderful, but when it doesn't, computer repair becomes a mental battle against another intelligence, something sentient, an intruder that you must remove.

To remove these intelligent viruses, you must interface with these machines with your human brain, as only the speed of thought is fast enough to go toe-to-toe with the mind of the machine. As such, computer repair is a risky business. Wrong moves can strain your mind. Too much strain is bad for business. Visualize the path to the heart of the computer faster than the intruder can, and expel it.

Each in-game day, customers enter the shop until you decide to close for the day, then you are given an opportunity to upgrade your shop. Take on as many as you think you can handle. However, if your neural strain hits 0, you lose all your rewards for the day, and must rest through the evening (not to mention the unhappy customer whose computer you failed to fix). **The day is the run.** Everything you close the day holding is yours for good, and closing is the only way it becomes yours, so every customer after the first is a bet on the ones you already took.

Six working days, then Sunday, and no last day at all. Shop upgrades help you earn more money, or help battle viruses more effectively. Most of them are not purchases so much as repairs: his solder bay, his onion router, his bench, all left broken. Your own rig is the same story. The neural deck you dive with was his, and it has more slots to unlock than it will ever have room for, so what you carry into a job is a choice you make against what the intake tells you is waiting in there.

The shop is in 2.5D, while computer repairs happen at the repair bench, where your [[kp-os]] powered computer helps you interface with the machines. You walk to the counter to meet someone, and you sit down at the bench to work.

Sundays are yours, and they are the only day you can challenge the backroom machine, so an attempt costs a day's takings whether you win or not. When you beat it, it is revealed that the virus was not a virus at all. It was an AI, [[patch]], built by your [[dad]], designed to keep you out until you proved you were ready. And then it is Monday, and the shop opens, and Patch is in the back room. Beating it is the largest thing in the game and it is not the end of it.

## The hook

**Upgrading or repairing the shop is how you learn who your father was.** Every upgrade helps you challenge harder dives, earn more money, gives you new abilities, gets you closer to being able to beat the backroom machine, and reveals more notes your father left when he was running the shop.

It is literal. You are not being told the story, you are clearing his clutter and finding him underneath it: the bills he filed under W, six years of pharmacy stubs in a shoebox, the lesson he taught you at a bench you had to rebuild before you could read about it. You fix what you can afford in whatever order you like, so it comes out of order, the way going through someone's things actually goes.

## The combat

A duel on a grid of scrambled pipe junctions. You rotate junctions to route your signal to the goal; so does the intrusion, on its own board. You can reach across and cut theirs. Rotation only turns one way, so undoing an enemy twist costs up to three times what making it did, and that exchange rate is the whole economy.

## The frame

The game is split between a 2.5D shop environment, and an operating system. KP/OS boots, you log in, you drag windows around. There is no menu layer over the game because the desktop **is** the core of the game. See [[kp-os]].

## Comparisons

The duel's read is closest to the Watch Dogs hacking minigame, turned into a two-sided race. The day-night structure is similar to Moonlighter, shopkeep and dive instead of shopkeep and dungeon crawl. The back room is a Stardew community center: you can open it early, and the game keeps going after you do.
