# Story overview

Spoiler-free. For what is actually true, see [[ground-truth]].

## The premise

Your father is dead. He left you his computer repair shop, counter and bench both, and a back room with a tower in it he never let you touch. The door is not locked. The machine is.

## The shape of it

The game opens once with the thing you cannot do: you sit down at the tower, you dive it, you are not ready, and it shuts the door. Then you go to work.

Customers come in with infected devices. You take as many as you dare in a day, dive them for pay, and close when your nerve or your [[neural-strain|strain]] runs out. What you close the day with, you keep. What you were still holding when you blacked out, you do not. Evenings are for putting the shop back together. On Sundays it is shut, and the tower is waiting.

## What the player is chasing

Two things at once, and they are the same thing:

- **Mechanically**, a shop and a deck good enough to beat the machine.
- **Narratively**, an answer to what the machine is and why he shut it.

## The engine of the story

> [!info] Fixing the shop is the delivery mechanism
> Every repair turns up something he left: a note, a bill, a receipt, a lesson. The player is not being told the story, they are clearing his clutter and finding him underneath it. See [[repairs-and-unlocks]].

There is no order to it. The player fixes what they can afford in whatever sequence they like, so every artifact stands alone. The one thing nothing says before the machine opens is what is inside it. See [[reveal-schedule]].

## The reveal, in outline

The debt, the illness, the nights in the back room, the recordings, the instruction. Assembled out of order, by someone alone, who started out certain it was a virus and has to talk himself out of that himself. A win delivers the last of it in the father's own voice, and then the shop opens again on Monday.

## Tone

A shop, not a city. The cyberpunk is entirely peripheral: courier drones and clinic gateways arrive as tickets on a spike. The story is grief, debt, and a soldering lesson.
