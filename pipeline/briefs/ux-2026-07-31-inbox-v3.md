## What the surface is

`windows/inbox.tsx` fronts the whole day loop and carries TWO states in one
window:

1. **LIST** (`run.screen === "day"`): the DAY hero strip, one boxed subject
   line per ticket with its threat pips and pay, the UP/DOWN hint, and a
   footer of run chips (STRAIN, CR, RAM, POUCH, KIT).
2. **CARD** (`run.screen === "analyze"`): the same list plus the expanded
   CUSTOMER.REC dossier and the DIVE / CONFIGURE KIT / BACK actions. The card
   currently carries NAME, DEVICE, DOMINANT ROUTINE, THREAT TIERS, TICKET
   RATE, GRID, INTRUSION RAM, an optional head-start WARNING, the INTAKE
   quote, the READOUT tell, two 1-bit photo cells, a printmark block, an eye
   crop, an oscilloscope trace and a twelve-row hex table.

The window opens at `frameW: 1210` and grows taller than a laptop desk when a
ticket opens, which is exactly the footprint problem law 3 exists to fix.

## The three problems, as constraints

1. **GLANCE ORDER IS UNDECIDED.** RULINGS.md section 11 names this the hard
   part: "two focal candidates". The DAY numeral is a hero today, and so is
   the dossier. Decide it in the spec and defend it in one sentence. The
   design position to argue against: this window is a DECISION surface, and
   the decision the player makes here is "what am I walking into, and is my
   kit configured for it" - which points at the dominant routine and the
   threat tier, not at the day number or the customer's name. If the spec
   disagrees, say why. Either way, ONE focal element per state, at extreme
   scale, with the type scale built to enforce it.
2. **FOOTPRINT.** Both states must clear the ~700px target and never exceed
   the 820px ceiling, measured on the tallest run state, at every supported
   viewport. The shipped open window is far over. The cuts discipline (law 8)
   is the tool, in its stated order, and every cut gets recorded.
3. **THE TWO-STATE PROBLEM.** Equal-footprint empty states (law 4) meets a
   window that today changes both width and height when a ticket opens. Spec
   what happens: if the width step survives it is a single `steps()`
   transition and the tiling claim is judged on the OPEN state; if it does
   not, the LIST state must not read as a mostly empty box. Both readings are
   legitimate; pick one and say which.

## What the study must be built to

Everything in RULINGS.md, in particular:

- Eight role tokens, copied verbatim from `loadout-eva/index.html`, with
  `data-scheme` NERV and TOKYO NIGHT. Never a hex value inside a component
  rule. `--r-warn` goes on exactly one thing.
- On this surface the obvious `--r-warn` candidate is the head-start WARNING
  (day 3+), and it must carry a second non-colour channel. THREAT TIER is NOT
  an alarm: it is live data (`--r-line`) with the pip row as its own second
  channel. Do not let the two share a colour.
- `clamp()` on `cqi`, one breakpoint at 700px, hard height ceiling, no
  internal scrollbars ever. The list is the obvious scroll temptation: three
  tickets is the shipped maximum, so it must simply fit.
- Flat CRT glass over the stage; FLAT and OFF only. Do not rebuild CURVED.
- `steps()` motion, compositor properties only, motion reserved for the
  alarm. The shipped open/close choreography (grow tall, then wide, then the
  card genies out of its row) is good and worth keeping; re-spec it against
  the performance law rather than dropping it.
- 1-bit imagery at 1:1, cropped never downscaled, framed on the SUBJECT.

## Variations the study must offer

1. **SCHEME**: NERV (default) / TOKYO NIGHT.
2. **CRT**: FLAT (default) / OFF.
3. **VIEWPORT**: 16:9 1366x768, 21:9 2560x1080, laptop 1280x800. All three
   render the SAME arrangement.
4. **RUN STATE**: at minimum a mid-run day with a full slate, a day 1 opener
   (sparse: no pouch, no augments, tier 1 across the board, no head start),
   and a late day where the head-start WARNING is live and at least one
   ticket is already CLEARED, so both the alarm and the struck-through
   equal-footprint row are reviewable rather than described.
5. **STATE**: an explicit switch between LIST and CARD, so the reviewer can
   judge both without hunting for the click.
6. Whatever treatment variation the dossier imagery wants, if the spec wants
   one (law 5's INK TINT / TRUE 1-BIT / FULL COLOUR row).
