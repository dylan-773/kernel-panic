# Ruling 22 - THE SHOP GOES NEON

*Added 2026-08-20, user directive. The 2026-08-19 overworld shipped reading as
the 1990s with the future left out: aged teal-gray plaster, tungsten lamp
pools, muted sprites. The user rejected it against a neon cyberpunk reference
(dense magenta, cyan and violet signage, holographic panels, backlit shelving,
purple-black base) and ruled the direction directly.*

## What it decided

The scene layer's visual register is neon cyberpunk. The operative law is
[[law-12-scene-art]]: the KP-NEON/16 palette, the lighting rules, and the
three prompt templates live there. This ruling records that the direction is
the user's, dated, and settled.

## Scope

1. **The scene layer only.** Room plates, state patches, walk sprites.
2. **KP/OS is untouched.** [[ruling-14-loadout-palette]]'s scope clause
   (multi-hue inside KP/OS limited to LOADOUT.CFG until the user says
   otherwise) stands exactly as written. This ruling is the user exercising
   the reserved multi-hue authority for the scene layer, not for KP/OS; the
   KP/OS half of [[palette-generalization-conflict]] stays open.
3. **The story register does not change.** [[story-overview]]'s "the
   cyberpunk is entirely peripheral" remains true as written: nobody in the
   fiction remarks on the neon, no street scene appears, the story stays
   grief, debt, and a soldering lesson. The neon is simply what the city
   outside looks like, bleeding in.

## Forbids

Scene-layer prompts built on the muted register (the words muted, 1990s,
cozy, moody), the closed muted hex set written for KP/OS pixel assets, or
any era descriptor that reads backward-only. Retro is half the brief; the
other half is futuristic.

## Permits

The current muted sprite set staying shipped as [[law-12-scene-art]]'s
compliant zero-accent case until a sprite pass regenerates it.
