# DARKNET.LNK

`darknet` · `windows/darknet.tsx`

The gray-market CLI. Night phase only. `notched: true` chrome.

## The sequence

1. **Dial** a handshake over **three relay hops**
2. Tonight's **blind-pull price**
3. **BUY**, then **escrow**
4. A **shape-scramble reveal**

Mechanics and pricing in [[the-darknet]].

## The interface is the character

> [!info] Canon forbids a person, so the window has to be one
> [[ruling-09-darknet]] makes the dealer **permanently anonymous**: no name, no ID, no relationship, no reveal, ever. There is nobody to write.
>
> So the characterization lives entirely in interface: the dial handshake makes the connection feel remote and effortful, the relay hops make it feel routed and deniable, the escrow makes it feel untrusting, and the scramble reveal makes the blindness of the roll into an event rather than a result.

A constraint that removed a character produced the most distinctive surface in the game.

## Odd one out via chrome, not colour

**Stepped-notch chrome** is DARKNET's identity, and it survived the v2 to v3 transition unchanged.

> [!warning] Keep it chrome, not colour
> An earlier design gave each window its own hue (magenta darknet, phosphor ledger, indigo dadlog). It was **killed** by the v2 redesign round-2 ruling, and [[ruling-14-loadout-palette]] explicitly declines to revive it.
>
> Distinguishing a window by silhouette rather than by hue is what lets [[law-1-colour-is-roles]] keep colour for state.
