# Game goals

> [!warning] status: draft
> The **what** and **how** are settled and read out of the shipped game. The **who** has never been written down anywhere in the repo. What follows is my reading of who the game is built for, based on what it is. Correct it.

## What

A finished, playable browser roguelite: tutorial, shop, OS, finale, story, sound and saves, all live at kernel-panic.higgsfield.app. Build 0.7.0.

It is also, in its second life, the artifact of a multi-agent development experiment. See [[the-dev-crew]] and [[token-budget]].

## Who

*Draft.* The game assumes a player who:

- will read a diagnostic before committing, and enjoys that the tell is honest;
- enjoys resource management, whether that's playing conservatively and never running out of neural strain, or pushing the limits of their skill and risking it all for that extra job reward;
- likes systems that are small enough to hold entirely in their head, yet grow in complexity as they progress
- enjoys autonomy and the difficult decision making that comes with it: can you handle a 5th customer today? should you upgrade the diagnostic bench first or unlock the dark net? are you ready to take on the machine in the back yet? nobody is there to give you the 'right answer,' which adds to the immersion of "my dad is gone and i have to fend for myself, this rides on me."

That points at the same audience as Moonlighter, Slay the Spire, and Persona. Moonlighter for the shopkeep-combat dynamic. Slay the spire for the roguelike, ability buildcrafting experience with a culminating boss fight. Persona for the overworld and metaverse hybrid, and day/night structure.
