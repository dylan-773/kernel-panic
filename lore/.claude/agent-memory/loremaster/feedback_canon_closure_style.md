When I proposed rulings for the 8 open canon questions (2026-07-25 session), on
two of the highest-stakes items the user went further than my recommendation
rather than picking one of my listed alternatives:

- Q1 (the mother): I recommended "deliberately left open" (never named, fate
  unknown, forbid future reveal). The user instead excised her from canon
  entirely: there is no "she", full stop, not even as an unresolved mystery.
- Q7 (timeline/dust): I recommended reconciling the "decade of dust" line by
  reading it as idiomatic (dust from years of secret nightly use, not time
  since death). The user instead removed dust as a motif entirely: the shop
  is buried in Dad's clutter, not dust, and this was ruled a rewrite of the
  shipped line rather than a reinterpretation of it.

**Why this matters:** the user is willing to rewrite already-shipped copy to
serve canon, not just reinterpret it creatively within existing text. When a
tension exists between two shipped lines, "which reading avoids contradicting
shipped copy" is a good default heuristic for a *proposal*, but the user may
still choose the harder cut: delete/rewrite the offending line rather than
find a clever reading that preserves it.

**How to apply:** next time I draft options for an open canon question,
include a plain "excise it / rewrite the shipped line" option explicitly,
even when a non-destructive reconciling reading exists, rather than treating
reconciliation as the obviously-safer default. Do not assume shipped copy is
sacrosanct when it conflicts with a cleaner canon; the user will greenlight a
copy-revision proposal (see [[project-2026-07-25-rulings]]) to fix it.
