# Ruling 17 - THE PLAYER'S OWN ASSUMPTION

*Decided 2026-08-16, user call, during the day-as-run redesign. Replaces the deleted ruling 2.*

**The player believes the back room holds a virus, and nobody told him so.** He inherited the assumption the way anyone inherits an assumption: it is the only thing it could reasonably be, given everything he knows about machines.

Nobody in the fiction ever asserted it. [[dad]] left no cover story, and there is no one else in the shop to have invented one.

**Forbids:** any line in which a character tells the player what is in the room; any framing of the belief as a lie, a deception or a thing being kept from him.

**Permits:** the belief standing as an honest, self-generated inference, which is what [[ruling-05-nature-of-intrusions]] exists to make reasonable. Living AI is the ordinary state of software in this world, and the ordinary explanation for a machine that fights back is a hostile one.

**Why it matters more than the version it replaces.** The doubt used to belong to a second person and the player watched it erode. Now the player has to argue himself out of his own position, with only what he digs out of his father's shop as evidence. There is nobody to be persuaded by and nobody to persuade. The renunciation is his, and it is authored as a [[sunday]] scene rather than delivered as dialogue.
